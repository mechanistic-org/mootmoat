Start with one decision you can explain and one canvas that fits it. The twelve techniques below are options, not a required sequence. Copy the prompts into your own notes and leave an honest gap wherever support is missing.

## Start with a common record

Use these fields with every canvas:

| Field | Your notes |
| --- | --- |
| Project and context | What was being built, when, and under which constraints? |
| Your role | What did you own? What did collaborators own? |
| Claim | What specific statement should a reader assess? |
| Source | Artifact, date/revision, relevant observation, and access conditions. |
| Evidence status | Recorded observation, recollection, inference, or proposal. |
| Limits | Missing evidence, uncertainty, alternative explanations. |
| Disclosure | What can be shared with this audience? |
| Next check | What would most improve confidence in the account? |

If you start from memory, label the draft accordingly. Return to available source material before treating its details as verified. Do not invent measurements to complete a field.

## 01. Provenance chain

Trace one feature through the failures and revisions that shaped it.

- **Current feature and project:** ...
- **Earlier failure or constraint:** ...
- **Decision or correction at each step:** ...
- **Your role at each step:** ...
- **Evidence for each link, including gaps:** ...

Do not turn a remembered sequence into a documented history. Mark links that still depend on recollection.

## 02. Structural rhyme

Compare a mechanism you know with a mechanism in another domain.

- **First domain and observed mechanism:** ...
- **Second domain and its supporting source:** ...
- **Shared constraint or failure mechanism:** ...
- **Conditions required for the comparison:** ...
- **Where the analogy breaks:** ...

Present the comparison as an analogy unless you can substantiate a stronger equivalence. Do not substitute a familiar metaphor for evidence.

## 03. Forensic number

Put a consequential measurement in its actual context.

- **Quantity, value, units, and uncertainty:** ...
- **Instrument or source record and date:** ...
- **Test conditions and sample:** ...
- **Baseline or comparison conditions:** ...
- **Decision the measurement informed:** ...

Use recorded precision. Label an estimate or remembered value. A decimal place does not make a claim more reliable.

## 04. Compressed war story

Draft one sentence that identifies a problem, decision, and supported result.

- **The essential sentence:** ...
- **The constraint behind the decision:** ...
- **Your contribution and collaborators:** ...
- **Evidence available for the result:** ...
- **What the short version leaves out:** ...

Keep a longer account behind the sentence. Check that compression has not removed a qualification or turned a team outcome into individual credit.

## 05. Constraint personality

Use a memorable description to explain a constraint you encountered.

- **Constraint and plain technical description:** ...
- **Behavior that surprised you:** ...
- **Event or observation behind the description:** ...
- **Metaphor you would use:** ...
- **Where the metaphor stops being useful:** ...

A metaphor is a teaching aid. Pair it with the actual mechanism and conditions so the reader does not mistake personality for a physical explanation.

## 06. Decision heuristic

Explain a rule you use, where it came from, and when it fails.

- **Rule in one sentence:** ...
- **Conditions where it applies:** ...
- **Origin event or supporting observation:** ...
- **Known exception or failure case:** ...
- **How you would check it on a new project:** ...

State the exception as carefully as the rule. One successful project does not establish a universal practice.

## 07. Counterfactual

Describe a risk you identified and the action taken to reduce it.

- **Observed condition:** ...
- **Predicted failure mode:** ...
- **Evidence or model supporting the prediction:** ...
- **Action and your role:** ...
- **What was observed afterward and what remains hypothetical:** ...

Distinguish observed outcomes from avoided-outcome estimates. Do not present an unobserved catastrophe or savings figure as a measured result.

## 08. Threshold vocabulary

Explain a term used to recognize a changing system state.

- **Term and plain-language meaning:** ...
- **Observable condition it describes:** ...
- **Threshold or criteria, if defined:** ...
- **Event that led to its use:** ...
- **Limits and possible misunderstanding:** ...

Define local jargon for an outside reader. Attribute a borrowed term; do not imply an established industry definition where there is none.

## 09. Tool as character

Explain why a particular tool helped you make a consequential decision.

- **Tool and task:** ...
- **Constraints that shaped the choice:** ...
- **Alternative considered:** ...
- **Assumptions you could inspect:** ...
- **Outcome and the tool's limitations:** ...

Describe a choice in context. Tool preference is not evidence that the tool is generally superior.

## 10. Negative-space expertise

Document a practice you avoid and the experience behind that boundary.

- **Practice you avoid:** ...
- **Specific conditions that make it risky:** ...
- **Origin event or supporting source:** ...
- **Exception where the practice may be reasonable:** ...
- **Alternative you use and how you check it:** ...

Replace a blanket prohibition with a scoped lesson. Repeat for up to three practices if the evidence warrants it.

## 11. Temporal signature

Explain why you address a problem at a particular project stage.

- **Problem:** ...
- **Stage where you intervene:** ...
- **Previous timing and observed consequences:** ...
- **Why the earlier or later timing helps:** ...
- **Evidence and costs of the timing decision:** ...

Compare with a documented project sequence. Avoid unsupported claims about what most engineers do.

## 12. Mentor echo

Describe advice that changed how you make a decision.

- **Advice, marked as quotation or paraphrase:** ...
- **Attribution, where appropriate to share:** ...
- **Context in which you received it:** ...
- **Decision where it proved useful:** ...
- **Limits or later revision of the advice:** ...

Mark remembered wording as a paraphrase unless verified. Preserve attribution and sharing permissions.

## Review before sharing

Read the draft against the [Source Standard and Depth Standard](/docs/doctrine/). Check the material claims, individual credit, conditions, and disclosure boundary. A missing source is a reason to qualify or remove a claim, not to embellish it.

Keep the working record and its revision history. Share the smallest account that supports the intended discussion, with clear limits and a path to further review where appropriate.
